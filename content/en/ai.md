LLMs are the biggest revolution in the IT field since... the internet? 

## AI in general

AI is really helpful to:
- research a specific topic;
- explain things that are hard to understand;
- teach you something you are not familiar with;
- find good recipes for cake;
- do many other things;
- and even write the code for your website (lol).

But AI shouldn't replace humans.

I (and many users online, I've discovered) don't like that people are completely relying on AI for entire projects. The number of repos with brand new software has dramatically increased lately, and much (if not all) of the code is written by LLMs. That's not the problem per se, but many of these people completely avoid saying who (or what) wrote what, while some even admit they never reviewed the code at all, pushing buggy, low-quality software out into the world. Maybe I'm biased, but I'm against that.

## AI in my projects

I use AI a **lot**. It helps me plan how to start my projects, clear up doubts and spot bad decisions, search through documentation more easily, and fix bugs.

## This site

The entire codebase is written by Claude. I'm not gonna lie.
I'm not a web developer or designer and that's not the reason for this website. But I reviewed every single decision, tested that everything works and kept it JS-free. It isn't something you install on your system, so the worst that can happen is a crooked border.

Although I'm good at English, it isn't my first language, so I use AI to pinpoint mistakes/fluency issues.
I even use it to translate all the pages from English to Italian, my native language
(because I want to write in English, but I can't be bothered to translate everything).

But `/content/en/*` is written by me with the ONLY exception of [security/anssi-linux.md](/security/anssi-linux/).
